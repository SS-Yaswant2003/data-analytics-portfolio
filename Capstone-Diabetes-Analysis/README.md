# 🩺 Diabetes Disease Data Analysis

A comprehensive data analytics project analyzing diabetes prevalence and associated demographic, lifestyle, clinical, and medical-history factors using **Microsoft Excel and Power BI**.

## 🎯 Project Objective

The objective was to analyze a large diabetes dataset and identify patterns in diabetes prevalence across demographic characteristics, lifestyle behaviors, clinical indicators, diabetes risk categories, and medical history.

The project transforms raw patient data into structured analysis and interactive dashboards to support data-driven insights.

## 📊 Dataset

The dataset contains **100,000 patient records** and **34 variables** covering:

- Ethnicity

## 🧹 Data Preparation

The dataset was prepared and analyzed using Microsoft Excel and SQL-based analysis techniques.

Data preparation included:

- Checking and handling missing values
- Identifying duplicate records
- Standardizing categorical values
- Creating age groups
- Creating diabetes status categories
- Creating diabetes risk categories
- Preparing the data for dashboard visualization

## 📊 Excel Analysis

The Excel analysis used PivotTables and summary analysis to examine:

1. Diabetes by Age Group
2. Diabetes by Gender
3. Average BMI by Diabetes Status
4. Average Hemoglobin by Diabetes Status
5. Average Glucose by Diabetes Status
6. Diabetes by Physical Activity
7. Diabetes by Smoking Status
8. Diabetes by Risk Category
9. Diabetes by Family History
10. Diabetes by Hypertension History

## 🔍 Key Findings

- The dataset contains **59,998 diabetic patients** and **40,002 non-diabetic patients**.
- The **56+ age group** contains the highest number of diabetic patients.
- Diabetes counts differ across gender categories.
- Average BMI is higher among diabetic patients compared with non-diabetic patients.
- Average fasting and postprandial glucose levels are higher among diabetic patients.
- Diabetes prevalence varies across physical activity and smoking categories.
- Higher diabetes risk categories contain a greater concentration of diabetic patients.
- Patients with a family history of diabetes show a different diabetes distribution compared with those without a family history.
- Diabetes distribution also varies based on hypertension history.
